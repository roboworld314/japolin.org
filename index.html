Selected research projects

BlindZoneEye: See Clearly, React Safely, and Record Reliably in Autonomous Driving

Under review at an IEEE journal

The problem

At occluded intersections and crosswalks, a vehicle often has to proceed, slow down, or yield while the conflict region still looks empty to its cameras and LiDAR. That makes blind-zone safety a decision-before-visibility problem, not only a harder detection task. Intersections account for about a quarter of U.S. road deaths.

How it works
  1. Detect. LiDAR occupancy captures static occlusions, raycasting finds line-of-sight blockage toward nearby road users, and YOLOv11 adds camera semantics. The cues fuse into a sector-level blind-zone risk score with a calibrated threshold.
  2. Mitigate. A high-risk sector triggers a cooperative perception message to a roadside unit, which fuses its own sensing and broadcasts a warning; the vehicle replans with model predictive control. HD maps infer blind zones when V2I is unavailable.
  3. Record. An event-aware black box keeps a rolling buffer and commits synchronized pre-, during-, and post-event evidence only when a blind-zone alert coincides with abnormal driving.
  • 41 ms detection latency, within a 100 ms safety budget
  • 86.8%+ detection accuracy held on the physical testbed
  • 96.7% event recall using 58.2% less storage than continuous logging

SenseGuard: Sensor-to-Decision Adaptation and Learning for Reliable Agentic Robots

Under review at an international robotics conference

The problem

An autonomy stack can report high accuracy and low per-frame latency and still act on the past. Redundant and vibration-corrupted frames queue up, so the model keeps processing stale inputs while the scene moves on. Across 20 detectors, a default full-rate pipeline trails a scene-current upper bound by 0.44 in present-world accuracy.

How it works
  1. Filter in time. SigLIP measures visual change, Qwen2.5-VL reasons over keyframes with structured chain-of-thought, and a PPO policy gives each camera frame bypass, reduced, or full fidelity, with a safety override for critical scenes. LiDAR scans use BEV histograms with VLM review for borderline cases.
  2. Reduce in space. Area-average downsampling for camera frames and vibration-calibrated voxel-grid filtering for LiDAR keep the geometry that localization needs.
  3. Learn from failures. A confidence monitor detects sustained degradation and commits a before, during, and after evidence capsule, so retraining concentrates on hard cases.
  • 31% lower inference latency
  • 75% lower LiDAR trajectory error (3.45 m to 0.87 m)
  • 136 ms to 63 ms decision age with semantic and RL admission

VibraBox: A Vibration-Adaptive Black Box for Replay and Learning from Failures in Mobile-Robot Autonomy

Under review at an international robotics conference

The problem

Robot failures are transient and hard to reproduce, so a robot needs a black box that keeps what it sensed before, during, and after an incident. Storage is tight, but how much evidence can safely be dropped depends on motion: a setting tuned on smooth floors breaks the replay-error bound on bumpy outdoor terrain.

How it works
  1. Capture continuously. A bounded ROS circular buffer keeps recent multimodal context, and a pluggable trigger, here blind-zone risk with a driving-anomaly gate, commits the pre-event window and records the aftermath.
  2. Reduce at three levels. Content-aware frame deduplication with BEV histograms, replay-calibrated voxel-grid downsampling, and fast lossless LZ4 or ZSTD compression in MCAP.
  3. Adapt in the loop. A DQN controller reads vibration, KISS-ICP scan-matching stability, and write backlog, then picks the threshold, voxel size, and codec for every scan to stay under the replay-error bound.
  • 14 runs indoors and outdoors, 32.34 GB of real vehicle data
  • 51.7% lower per-frame pipeline latency than the fixed setting
  • +12.7% recall and −35.2% false positives after retraining on retained events

Log What Matters: Safety-Aware Adaptive Logging for CAVs in Dynamic Conditions

IEEE MOST 2026, Detroit

Read the paper

The problem

Real vehicle sensor streams are full of redundant, low-value frames. They waste compute and I/O, push decisions onto stale perception, and bias training toward easy, clear-weather scenes.

How it works
  1. Log camera frames by value. SigLIP, Qwen-VL, and a PPO policy keep, downsize, or drop each frame by its safety value.
  2. Log LiDAR scans by geometry. BEV histograms and a DQN agent tuned by KISS-ICP stability remove duplicate scans.
  3. Choose the sensor by context. Qwen-VL reads the weather, and GPT-4o weighs camera and LiDAR reliability.

Context-Aware Perception: VLM-Augmented All-Weather Detection for Autonomous Driving

ACM/IEEE SEC 2025, Arlington, VA

Read the paper

The problem

Rain, fog, and snow hide objects, so detectors trained on clear weather fail when they matter most. Across 11 detectors on BDD100K, fog hurt most, and RF-DETR and YOLOv10-M were the most robust.

How it works
  1. Context-aware annotation. QwenVL labels weather and visibility and writes a scene description for each frame.
  2. Context-aware augmentation. QwenVL-guided rendering adds realistic rain, fog, and snow to balance the data.
  3. Reliability-driven sensor choice. A reliability score and GPT-4o reasoning pick the most trustworthy sensor.
